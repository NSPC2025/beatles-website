This acoustic snippet - only forty-three seconds long - opens the McCartney album, capturing its naïf approach. It is emblematic that the song is dedicated to Linda Eastman, the first of Paul's three wives. While she was not taken seriously by music critics, Linda has always represented a beacon of reference, a psychological support and a source of inspiration for McCartney to compose a great many songs.

"The Lovely Linda" was committed to tape to test the recording equipment. Paul: "Written in Scotland, this was the first song I recorded when the Studer 4-track was installed at home." This recording - which includes vocals and acoustic guitar (track 1), another acoustic guitar (track 2), percussion achieved by drumming on a book (track 3) and bass (track 4) - also features the noises captured at home and ends with a hiccupping laughter, almost to prove the semi-serious tone of the album venture. Paul: "The record is me playing around the house. You hear Linda walking through the living room doorway out to the garden, and the door squeaks at the end of the tape."

The song is the portrait of a carefree, happy moment for the couple. Paul: "She often used to wear flowers in her hair, so it's a direct diary." As Paul said at the time, this recording was an anticipation of the full version of "The Lovely Linda", recorded perhaps shortly thereafter and completed with a fragment in Mariachi style; if this recording exists, it remains, to date, unreleased.

The orchestral version, recorded at Abbey Road and included on Working Classical - an album released in 1999 and including remakes in classical style of several solo songs of McCartney's career together with some new compositions for orchestra - is performed by the Loma Mar Quartet, an ensemble consisting of Krista Bennion Feeney (first violin), Anca Nicolau (second violin), Joanna Hood (viola) and Myron Lutzke (cello). McCartney played "The Lovely Linda" on acoustic guitar for the TV documentary accompanying the collection Wingspam - Hits and History (2001), a retrospective on Wings, the group founded by McCartney in summer 1971.
