The first album conceived, recorded and released by McCartney after his decision to talk publicly about the situation regarding The Beatles (if not yet a formal breakup of the band, certainly an interruption in any partnership with John, George and Ringo), RAM was intended to be Paul's tour de force. Whereas McCartney was released along with the announcement of Paul's leaving the group but was recorded with The Beatles still formally together, RAM represented the release of all the tension that built up in McCartney after such a difficult decision. For these reasons, RAM had the same emotional importance and significance for McCartney that Plastic Ono Band had for Lennon.

Paul: "I was going through a bad time, what I suspect was almost a nervous breakdown. I remember lying awake at nights shaking ... One night I'd been asleep and awoke and I couldn't lift my head off the pillow. My head was down in the pillow, I thought, 'Jesus, if I don't do this I'll suffocate'."

The album was conceived almost by accident, with no precise aim, and McCartney was assembled relying both on home recordings and songs recorded in professional studios. The sessions took place on several separate occasions and studios. The recordings started "around Christmas" in 1969, after McCartney returned from his vacation in Antigua. Paul began recording in a room in his house at 7 Cavendish Avenue in St. John's Wood, London, relying on his Studer J37 4-track recorder. Adopting a layman's approach and without even using a mixer, he played all instruments himself: Martin D-28 acoustic guitar, Fender Telecaster 1966 and Epiphone Casino 1962 electric guitars, Rickenbacker 4001s bass and a Ludwig drum kit, which combines two of Ringo's drum sets.

Since Les Paul (the pseudonym of Lester William Polfuss) had invented multitrack recording (the guitarist started his experiments in 1945 and in 1948 released the single "Lover", with eight different guitar parts superimposed), there weren't many artists, famous or lesser known, who hadn't at some stage recorded (almost) everything on their own. Paul: "I had this 4-track machine, and I just had it in the living room in the corner - I had this very simple technique of plugging in directly, right into the back of the machine which was a very cool way to record ... so pure. The sound - there is nothing purer. I like that primitive way of recording."

The process needed some effort to be mastered. Paul: "Playing with myself was easy, because I knew what I was thinking - the only trouble, which was a minor one, was that in order to keep time it was often necessary to do drums first (on their own) - and that was sometime a bit hard - but fun."

For the vocals, Paul was only joined by his wife Linda who added backing vocals on four songs. In all, 13 tracks were recorded. In addition to the eleven tracks later included on the album, McCartney also taped another two (as yet unreleased) songs: "Backwards Guitar Piece" and "Rupert (Guitar)", the last one conceived for a musical project related to Rupert Bear and re-recorded during the subsequent sessions for the RAM album, under the title "When the Wind Is Blowing". Not counting a short spoken bit called "Don't Cry Baby", with Paul trying to comfort little Mary.

Shortly after the recording of "Maybe I'm Amazed" in a single session on 15 February at Abbey Road, McCartney moved to Morgan Studios in Willesden. Between 16 and 20 February, assisted by engineer Robin Black, Paul recorded "Kreen-Akrore" (with Linda on backing vocals) and worked on some instrumental overdubbing of the home recordings, also carrying out some mixing. Another singer was recording in the same studio in those days, Donovan Leitch, of whom McCartney was an acquaintance. Donovan: "Downstairs was Paul doing his solo album. He gave me his Stratocaster, which I used for the song 'Changes' [later included on the album Open Road]."

Work on the album was concluded between 22 and 25 February at Abbey Road where Paul, who booked the studios under the pseudonym Billy Martin, did the mixing for the songs previously taped at home, and recorded "Every Night" and "Man We Was Lonely" – with Linda again on backing vocals on the latter two – under the technical supervision of Phil McDonald, assisted by Alan Parsons, Chris Thomas and John Kurlander, all having worked already on Abbey Road.

Kurlander: "Paul was relaxed, bearded, cheerful. Linda didn't sing or perform, but she had opinions about what she was hearing, and Paul was obviously listening to what she said. It was definitely a family business now."

John Leckie worked as a tape operator, whose duties consisted of threading audio tape, setting up microphones, stands and cables. Leckie: "The first thing I did with any of The Beatles was McCartney's first solo album [later on he would work with Harrison on All Things Must Pass and Lennon on John Lennon/Plastic Ono Band]. I was really a tape op, I can't say I was responsible for much of the production of the record. At that time there were no remotes for the tape machines, so every time they wanted to do anything, they would shout at you - 'Start!', 'Stop!', 'Go back to the second verse', and you had to be really quick. If Paul McCartney is saying, 'Drop me in on the second verse', you have to know where it is. It was a one-inch, eight-track recording, which meant you had to plan where everything was going, you couldn't just throw it down."
